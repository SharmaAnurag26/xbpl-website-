Cloud migration is not a single project. It is a change in how technology is designed, operated and paid for. These five considerations make the difference between a migration that simply relocates problems and one that creates lasting value.

## 1. Know what you have

A reliable inventory of applications, dependencies, data flows and owners is the foundation of every good migration plan. Discovery often reveals forgotten integrations and systems that can be retired rather than moved.

## 2. Choose the right path for each workload

Not every application should be migrated the same way. Common options include:

- **Rehost**: move as-is for speed, then optimise later.
- **Replatform**: make targeted changes, such as moving to a managed database.
- **Refactor**: redesign for cloud-native services where the business case supports it.
- **Replace or retire**: adopt a SaaS alternative, or switch the system off.

## 3. Design security and governance up front

Identity, network segmentation, encryption, logging and policy guardrails are far easier to build into a landing zone than to retrofit. Define who can create resources, how environments are separated, and how compliance is evidenced before the first workload moves.

## 4. Plan for cost from the start

Cloud spending follows usage, which is both its strength and its risk. Tagging standards, budgets, rightsizing and clear accountability for spend help avoid surprises. Treat cost as an engineering metric, reviewed as regularly as performance.

## 5. Prepare your people and operations

New platforms need new skills and ways of working: infrastructure as code, automated deployments, observability and incident response in a shared-responsibility model. Investing in team capability early reduces risk and speeds up every subsequent wave of migration.
