Most organizations have now run a generative AI pilot. Far fewer have turned those pilots into capabilities the business relies on every day. The gap is rarely the model itself. It is everything around it: the use case, the data, the people and the controls.

## Start with a business problem, not a model

Successful programmes begin with a narrow, valuable problem: drafting responses for a support team, summarising long documents for analysts, or accelerating code reviews. A clear problem makes it possible to define what "good" looks like and to measure it.

Useful questions to ask before building anything:

- What decision or task will this improve, and for whom?
- How is that task measured today (time, quality, cost, risk)?
- What happens when the output is wrong, and who catches it?

## Get the data foundations right

Generative AI is only as useful as the context it can draw on. Retrieval over well-governed internal content usually matters more than model choice. That means knowing where authoritative information lives, keeping it current, and enforcing the same access permissions in the AI experience as in the source systems.

## Design for people, not just prompts

Adoption depends on trust. Teams need to understand what the tool is good at, where it is weak, and how to check its work. Short, role-specific enablement (with real examples from their own workflows) consistently outperforms generic training.

## Build guardrails in from day one

Moving to production means addressing:

1. **Security and privacy**: what data can be sent where, and how it is logged.
2. **Quality**: evaluation sets that reflect real tasks, reviewed regularly.
3. **Governance**: clear ownership, an approval path for new use cases, and an inventory of what is in use.

## Scale what works

Once a use case proves its value, the patterns behind it (retrieval pipelines, evaluation harnesses, access controls and enablement material) become reusable building blocks. That is when experimentation turns into enterprise value: each new use case becomes faster and safer to deliver than the last.
