Attackers adapt as quickly as the organizations they target. As businesses adopt cloud services, remote work and AI, the attack surface grows, and so does the need for security that is designed in rather than bolted on.

## Identity is the new perimeter

With applications spread across cloud and SaaS platforms, user and machine identities are now the most common route in. Phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication, least-privilege access and regular reviews of privileged accounts remain some of the highest-impact controls available.

## Ransomware and extortion keep evolving

Modern ransomware operations often combine data theft with encryption, adding pressure through the threat of publication. Effective defences include:

- tested, isolated backups that can be restored quickly;
- network segmentation to limit how far an intruder can move;
- rapid patching of internet-facing systems;
- a rehearsed incident response plan.

## The supply chain is part of your attack surface

Software dependencies, managed service providers and SaaS integrations can all introduce risk. Understanding which third parties have access to what, and monitoring that access, is now a core security discipline.

## AI cuts both ways

Generative AI makes convincing phishing and social engineering cheaper to produce. It also helps defenders triage alerts, summarise incidents and spot anomalies faster. Organizations should update awareness programmes and set clear policies for how AI tools may be used internally.

## Building resilience, not just defences

No organization can prevent every incident. Resilience comes from combining prevention with detection and response:

1. **Visibility**: centralised logging and monitoring across endpoints, cloud and identity.
2. **Detection**: use cases tuned to your environment, reviewed as threats change.
3. **Response**: clear roles, playbooks and regular exercises.
4. **People**: ongoing awareness and capability building across the workforce.
